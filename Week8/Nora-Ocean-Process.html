Ocean Mapping: Jellyfish and Pixel Tide

Creative Coding · Personal process documentation by Nora

Project idea

For my part of the ocean projection project, I developed two animations: a jellyfish for a vase and a pixel tide for a reflective balloon. I wanted to explore how the shape and material of an everyday object could become part of the animation.

My starting point was Zima Blue. I was interested in using a strong blue within a digital visual language. For this project, I focused on a reflective balloon and a vase. I wanted the balloon to carry an expanding and disappearing sea, and the vase to become a small habitat for a jellyfish.

My process: the tide

I first explored a sea made of small text and data-like marks. When I projected the detailed pattern onto the smooth foil balloon, the image was difficult to read. However, its reflected light reminded me of gentle light shimmering on the sea. I decided to use the material itself to create atmosphere. The balloon already had a square pattern, so I moved toward larger square pixels. I kept the water #0022FF, made the foam white, and left the space outside the water black. The tide now travels beyond the circular mask in both directions, so it can completely fill the balloon area and then disappear.

The code builds the waterline from a slow rise-and-fall cycle and two smaller waves. It fills the area below that line with blue blocks. A few rows of white blocks form the foam. The balloon test changed the purpose of this animation. I used a larger change in the bright area to create moving ambient light around the installation, rather than rely on small details remaining visible on the surface.

My process: the jellyfish

I wanted the vase to feel like a small ocean, with a jellyfish swimming naturally inside it. I started with a MATLAB adaptation of mathematical art by @yuruyurau on X. The code places thousands of points using sine and cosine, then changes their positions over time. I tried changing its proportions and adding detail, but several versions lost the delicate shape that I liked. I returned to a simpler adaptation of the formula.

The original mathematical artwork is by @yuruyurau. Mike Croucher describes a MATLAB adaptation in The MATLAB Blog. My version modifies this reference for a p5.js animation and the vase projection. The original formula, the MATLAB adaptation and my changes are separate contributions.

Preparing my animations for projection

I prepared the jellyfish as a rectangular texture and placed the tide inside a circular mask for the balloon. Each animation draws into its own offscreen canvas, which p5.mapper displays on a projection surface. This lets me adjust the placement of the projection while the animation keeps running.

Reflection

The hardest part for me was deciding which details actually helped the image. Adding more detail often made the jellyfish less convincing. With the tide, the clearest decisions were a strong blue, a white edge and a much larger change in water level. This process helped me see that I need to judge movement and shape together, rather than adjust individual parameters without looking at the whole image.

Testing the balloon showed me that the material could change the direction of the design. The image lost detail, but the reflection suggested a more useful role: creating a shifting atmosphere around the other objects. My next step is to record the jellyfish on the vase and the tide’s reflections in the surrounding space, so I can compare the projected result with the screen version.
