Project Introduction

My favorite web-based project is The Boat.

Theme

The Boat is an interactive web comic adapted from Nam Le's fictional short story of the same name.

The story is set against the historical background of the end of the Vietnam War in 1975, when Saigon—now Ho Chi Minh City—fell and Vietnam was reunified. In the years that followed, many people chose to leave Vietnam because of political circumstances and concerns about life and safety after the war. Without regular ways to leave the country, many were forced to crowd onto small boats that were not designed for long ocean journeys. They crossed the South China Sea in the hope of reaching Southeast Asian countries or eventually being accepted by a third country. They later became known as the Vietnamese boat people.

The Boat follows Mai, a sixteen-year-old Vietnamese girl who is forced to separate from her family and join more than two hundred other people on a crowded boat. Drifting across the sea, they try to reach a safer place. During the journey, Mai meets a woman named Quyen and her young son, Truong. As they face storms, diminishing supplies, illness, and other dangers, Mai goes through an ordeal involving hope, survival, care for others, and loss.

Narrative Structure

Traditional comics tell stories through panels. Whether they are printed page comics or vertical webtoons, they usually rely on still images to create dramatic developments and transitions. The Boat also uses scrolling as its main form of narrative progression, but its web-based interactive effects create a much stronger sense of immersion.

The visual storytelling mainly works on two levels. The background establishes the overall setting: it shows the larger environment, such as a storm at sea or the intense heat of the sun. At the same time, it reflects the protagonists changing emotional state. Abstract lines suggest fear, suffering, and inner torment, while brighter images and the disappearing boat convey both the relief of finally seeing land and the quiet helplessness of losing companions.

On top of these backgrounds, comic panels depict specific actions and conversations between characters. This makes it possible to portray emotional changes between people in greater detail. The characters in these panels are often still, while the background continues to move and change with the plot. As a result, the audience can follow the story more deeply through Mai's perspective.

For example, when Mai's boat moves away from the shore, scrolling causes the small boat in the center of the screen to travel forward. When the person steering the boat lights a lamp in a panel, a light also appears at the bow of the boat in the background. The timing is very precise: the rhythm of each visual element is closely connected to the narrative.

Similarly, when the boat encounters a storm, the entire composition may suddenly tilt strongly in one direction or shake violently. These movements make the reader feel more physically present in the scene.

Images and Text

There are three main types of text in the project: descriptive narration, dialogue inside speech bubbles, and text used to create particular effects —— For example, rough handwritten lettering makes a song feel distant and dreamlike.

Each type of text has a consistent visual form, which makes them easy to distinguish. At the same time, as Mai's mental state changes, some of the text begins to blend into the background and becomes difficult to read. This reflects her blurred consciousness as she suffers from dehydration and hunger at sea.

Reflection

The Boat transforms scrolling from a simple reading action into something that feels like personally moving through a dangerous, long, and unstoppable escape across the sea. Although the project is quite long, its interactions rarely feel repetitive. Even when it uses simple combinations of existing elements, the experience remains engaging. I find this especially impressive.

Overall, The Boat is an excellent reference for future narrative web interactions. It shows how interactive web design can support not only visual presentation, but also storytelling, emotion, and the reader's experience of a character's journey.
